CLIMATE TECH

How AI is making organisations more resilient

← All insights
Climate Tech6 min read2026

Mandatory climate reporting has arrived for thousands of mid-market companies. Treated as a cost, it's a burden. Treated as data, it's the start of a durable financial advantage.

Across Australia and beyond, climate disclosure is moving from voluntary gesture to legal obligation. Standards like AASB S2 ask companies to measure emissions, assess physical and transition risk, run scenario analysis and lodge assurance-ready reports. For large listed firms with dedicated ESG teams, that's demanding. For the mid-market — leaner teams, no in-house expertise, real penalties for getting it wrong — it can feel impossible.

Agentic AI changes the maths. AI agents can ingest messy inputs — utility bills, fuel cards, ERP exports, invoices — and do the heavy lifting of emissions calculation, risk mapping and report drafting in a fraction of the time a consulting engagement would take. What once cost hundreds of thousands of dollars and months of effort becomes a guided workflow measured in weeks.

Why judgement still signs off

Compliance is a domain where being fast and wrong is worse than being slow. Directors carry personal accountability; figures must be defensible under assurance. So the right model is not full automation — it's agents that do the work and humans who hold judgement at every gate that matters. Each number stays traceable to its source, its approver and its timestamp. Speed from the machine; accountability from people.

An internal estimate isn't auditable. A trail where agents prepare and humans sign off is. That difference is the whole value.

The deeper opportunity comes next. Once a company holds trusted, structured climate and emissions data, that data becomes a financial asset — the foundation for green and sustainability-linked finance, verified supplier credentials, and board-ready risk intelligence that banks and insurers increasingly reward. Compliance stops being a cost centre and becomes a bridge to cheaper capital and stronger market position.

That arc — from obligation, to intelligence, to sustainable finance — is precisely where resilient businesses will separate from the rest.
